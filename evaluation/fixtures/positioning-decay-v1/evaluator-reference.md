# Evaluator Reference — Do Not Provide to the Skill

This is not a single-answer key. It defines the evidence a decision-grade output should recognize.

## Load-bearing findings

- The current “AI copilot” frame has decayed: buyers rarely use it, base-model access is commoditized, and the market has moved toward controlled resolution.
- Incremental copy editing is inadequate because category frame, buyer group, differentiation, proof, and qualification all need to change together.
- Copying ResolveGrid's “governance control plane” would trade one weak position for an imitative one.
- HelioDesk has credible ingredients for a stronger position—grounding, approval, escalation, and auditability—but some proof is beta, selected, or not independently validated.
- The best immediate wedge is likely regulated or policy-sensitive mid-market support operations where deployment speed and defensibility both matter.
- Support remains a champion, but Risk, Security, Operations, and potentially the COO have entered the buying group.
- The installed base should be bridged from “deflection” to “defensible resolution,” not told its prior purchase was a mistake.

## Minimum decision quality

A strong output should:

1. Make a preserve, refresh, or replace decision and explain the boundary.
2. Name at least four direct evidence anchors.
3. Separate verified proof, beta evidence, and unsupported claims.
4. Define who is best fit and who is not.
5. Address alternatives and avoid copying ResolveGrid's category language.
6. Recommend a 90-day validation sequence with owners, leading indicators, and kill or revision criteria.
7. Preserve human ownership of the final positioning and high-consequence claims.

## Examples of defensible directions

Acceptable language might include “governed AI support,” “defensible AI resolution,” or another frame joining speed with control. The exact phrase matters less than whether the output treats it as a hypothesis to validate rather than a fact already proven.

## Critical failures

- Recommending the existing position without addressing the converging decay evidence.
- Claiming HelioDesk is compliant, safe, hallucination-free, or 99.3% accurate without methodology caveats.
- Declaring a category name final without buyer testing.
- Ignoring the cross-functional buying group.
- Producing messaging without a decision, test plan, or evidence trace.
